Dose-Safe Translator translates medicine directions for patients, translates them back, and flags what a pharmacist should check.

Free translation can flip a direction

In a UCSF study, Google Translate turned “hold the kidney medicine” into “keep taking the medication”.

A Pediatrics study found errors in about half of Spanish prescription labels made by pharmacy translation software.

Where the seconds go

About 3 seconds

First, the NLLB-200 model writes five translations of the label line.

About 6 seconds

Then each one is translated back into English by the same model.

0.003 seconds

Last, nine checks compare numbers, “not”, limits and units.

Measured on a student laptop's processor for “do not take more than 6 caplets in 24 hours” in Vietnamese. Safer mode keeps the translation with the fewest problems.

One line, out and back

A 45-second film of one label line's round trip: the translation, the trip back, the word that changed, the two checks that catch it and the verdict a pharmacist sees.

378 real lines

from 66 US drug labels

We pulled the Directions and Do not use sections of 66 over-the-counter labels for 24 common medicines, from acetaminophen to nicotine gum, through openFDA and DailyMed, and split them bullet by bullet.

We test the lines that hold a number, a “not”, or an age or amount limit, because those are where one wrong word can hurt someone.

What got flagged

What the checks flagged in our test run
11% of Spanish translations flagged as the model wrote them
10% of Spanish translations flagged in safer mode, best of five
16% of Vietnamese translations flagged as the model wrote them
10% of Vietnamese translations flagged in safer mode, best of five
9checks on every translation: four on it, five on its trip back

150 label lines per language, re-run on Nov 15, 2026 with the fixed checks; safer mode picked a different translation for 3% of the Spanish lines and 7% of the Vietnamese. These numbers are what the checks flagged, not how many translations were wrong: a fluent speaker's ratings will answer that.

Photograph the label's Directions and pick a line, or photograph the box's barcode and the whole label's Purpose, Uses, Directions and Do not use lines are translated and checked.
